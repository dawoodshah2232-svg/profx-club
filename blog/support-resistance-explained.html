Support and Resistance, Simply Explained

August 28, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Support and resistance are the most practical concepts in technical analysis — zones where price repeatedly turns.

The core idea

Support is a zone where buying has repeatedly overcome selling, halting declines.

Resistance is the mirror: selling overcomes buying, capping rallies. They form because traders remember these levels and act on them — a self-fulfilling, genuinely useful phenomenon.

How to mark them

On a clean chart, mark zones (not exact lines) where price reversed at least twice. Prioritise higher timeframes — a weekly level outweighs an hourly one.

The more touches without breaking, the stronger the zone. Fewer, cleaner levels beat a chart full of lines.

How price behaves there

Price can bounce, consolidate, or break through.

Breakouts often retest the broken level (old resistance becomes new support). False breaks — quick spikes beyond the zone that reverse — are common, which is why patient traders wait for confirmation.

Trading with S/R

Common approaches: buy near support with a stop just below, sell near resistance with a stop just above, or trade the breakout after a retest.

Whatever you choose, the level gives your stop-loss a logical home — which is half the battle.

Key takeaways

  • Mark zones with 2+ touches; higher timeframes matter more.
  • Expect bounces, breaks, retests — and false breaks.
  • Levels give your stop-loss a logical placement.

Frequently asked questions

Lines or zones?

Zones — price rarely respects an exact pip. Give levels breathing room.

What if price blows through my level?

Then the market disagreed — honour your stop. Broken support often becomes resistance; reassess.

How many levels should I draw?

A handful of major ones. Cluttered charts produce cluttered decisions.
