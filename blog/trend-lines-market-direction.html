Trend Lines: Spotting Market Direction

August 27, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Trend lines are diagonal support/resistance that reveal market direction at a glance. Simple tool, real edge when used well.

Drawing them right

An uptrend line connects rising swing lows (at least two, three is better); a downtrend line connects falling swing highs.

Use wicks or bodies consistently, and never force a line to fit — if it needs forcing, there is no trend line.

What trends tell you

Price above a rising trend line: buyers in control — favour long ideas.

Price breaking the line: momentum may be shifting. Sideways: no trend — trend strategies sit out. 'The trend is your friend' works because trends persist more often than they reverse.

Trend line breaks

Breaks happen constantly; most are noise.

Higher-probability breaks come with: a strong candle close beyond the line, increased volume, and a failed retest. Beginners should wait for the retest rather than chasing the break candle.

Combining with horizontal levels

Where a trend line meets a horizontal support/resistance zone, you get confluence — two reasons for price to react.

These meeting points are among the highest-quality areas on any chart. Mark them; wait for them.

Key takeaways

  • Connect 2–3 swing points; never force a line.
  • Trade with the trend; sit out sideways markets.
  • Wait for break + retest instead of chasing breakouts.

Frequently asked questions

Which timeframe for trend lines?

Draw on the timeframe you trade, but confirm the bigger trend one timeframe up.

How steep is too steep?

Very steep lines break fast and mean little. Sustainable trends have moderate slopes.

Do trend lines work on gold?

Yes, but gold's volatility means more false breaks — require stronger confirmation.
