Candlestick Basics: Reading Price Charts

August 29, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Candlesticks are the language of price charts. Each candle tells you who won the battle — buyers or sellers — in that period.

Anatomy of a candle

The body shows open-to-close: green (or white) if price closed higher, red (or black) if lower. The wicks show the full high-low range.

A long body means conviction; long wicks mean rejection of those prices. Four numbers, one picture.

What candles reveal

A series of rising green candles with small wicks shows steady buying; long upper wicks at highs show sellers pushing back.

Candles do not predict — they describe the current balance of power, which is exactly what you need to read.

A few patterns worth knowing

Beginners should recognise: doji (indecision), hammer (rejection of lows), engulfing (strong reversal signal), and long-legged indecision at extremes.

Learn five patterns deeply rather than fifty superficially — context matters more than names.

Candles in context

A hammer at strong support means something; a hammer in the middle of nowhere means little.

Always read candles with location: trend, support/resistance, and session. Pattern without context is astrology with extra steps.

Key takeaways

  • Body = open-to-close conviction; wicks = rejection extremes.
  • Learn 5 patterns deeply instead of 50 superficially.
  • Patterns only matter at meaningful locations.

Frequently asked questions

Which timeframe's candles should I learn on?

Start with H1 or H4 — cleaner than frantic M1 noise, faster feedback than daily.

Do candlestick patterns really work?

They describe supply and demand; their edge comes from context and confirmation, not magic.

Green/red or white/black?

Colour scheme is personal preference — the information is identical.
