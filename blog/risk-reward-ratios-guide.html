Risk-Reward Ratios: Plan Every Trade

September 17, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Risk-reward ratio compares what you risk to what you aim to gain. It is how traders stay profitable even when they lose half their trades.

The basic math

Risk $10 to make $20 and your ratio is 1:2. Win just 40% of such trades and you still profit: 4 wins × $20 = $80 against 6 losses × $10 = $60.

This is why professionals obsess over ratios, not win rates. A 90% win rate with terrible ratios still loses money.

Setting it before entry

Decide your stop and target before you enter, then check the ratio.

If the chart only offers 1:1 for your setup, skip the trade — chasing poor ratios is slow-motion account erosion. Patience for good ratios is a genuine trading edge.

Realistic targets

Targets must respect the market's structure: place them at logical support/resistance, not at fantasy numbers that give a nice ratio.

A 'perfect' 1:3 to a price the market will never reach is worthless. Honest ratios beat pretty ratios.

Tracking your average

Journal every trade's planned and actual risk-reward.

Over 50+ trades you will see your true average — then you know exactly what win rate you need to break even. That number turns trading from guessing into a measurable business.

Key takeaways

  • At 1:2, a 40% win rate is profitable — ratios beat win rates.
  • Set stop and target before entry; skip poor-ratio trades.
  • Base targets on market structure, not wishful numbers.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good risk-reward ratio?

Many traders aim for 1:2 or better. Below 1:1.5, you need a very high win rate to survive.

Should I always use take-profit orders?

Planning exits in advance is essential; whether via limit orders or manual management depends on your style.

Can a high win rate overcome bad ratios?

Rarely. One oversized loss wipes out dozens of tiny wins — the classic scalping trap.
