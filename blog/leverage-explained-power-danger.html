Leverage Explained: Power and Danger

September 19, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small deposit. It is the most misunderstood tool in forex — powerful, and dangerous in untrained hands.

How leverage works

With 1:100 leverage, $100 of margin controls a $10,000 position. The broker lends you the buying power; your deposit is the security.

A 1% market move then equals 100% of your margin — in either direction. That multiplication is the whole story.

A concrete example

You have $500 and open a $50,000 EUR/USD position at 1:100. A 50-pip adverse move (≈0.46%) loses about $230 — nearly half your account.

The same trade at 1:10 leverage would risk a tenth of that. Leverage did not change the market; it changed what the market could do to you.

Why beginners get hurt

High leverage makes tiny market noise feel dramatic, triggering emotional decisions: moving stops, doubling down, revenge trading.

Brokers offering 1:500 or 1:1000 are not doing you a favour — they are selling rope. Many experienced traders use effective leverage under 1:10.

Using leverage safely

Keep effective leverage low, risk 1% per trade, and always use a stop-loss.

Think of leverage as a power tool: in trained hands it is efficient; in careless hands it causes the injury. If you cannot explain your leverage ratio, you should not be trading live.

Key takeaways

  • Leverage multiplies losses exactly as much as gains.
  • A 0.5% market move at 1:100 leverage ≈ 50% of margin.
  • Keep effective leverage low and always use a stop-loss.

Frequently asked questions

What leverage do professionals use?

Typically low effective leverage, often under 1:10. High headline leverage is a marketing tool, not a strategy.

Does leverage affect the spread?

No, but it magnifies what every pip — including the spread cost — is worth to your account.

Should beginners use 1:500 leverage?

No. It turns normal market noise into account-threatening swings.
