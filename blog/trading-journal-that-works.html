Keeping a Trading Journal That Works

September 11, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

A trading journal records every trade and the reasoning behind it. It is the highest-return habit in trading — and it costs nothing.

What to record

For each trade: date, pair, direction, entry, stop, target, size, setup name, and — crucially — your emotional state and whether you followed your plan.

Screenshots of the chart at entry and exit make reviews ten times more useful. Five minutes per trade is enough.

The weekly review

Once a week, read your journal asking one question: did I follow my process?

Categorise mistakes (chased entry, moved stop, oversized) and tally them. You will discover your losses come from a handful of repeated behaviours — and behaviours can be fixed.

What the journal reveals

Most traders find their strategy is fine but their discipline is not: the journal proves it with numbers.

It also reveals your best setups, best sessions, and emotional patterns (e.g. 'I trade badly after two losses'). Data beats memory — memory lies, journals don't.

Keep it simple enough to sustain

A spreadsheet or notebook you actually use beats fancy software you abandon.

If journaling feels like a chore, simplify it: even entry/stop/target/size/emotion on one line per trade transforms your learning speed.

Key takeaways

  • Record setup, levels, size, plan-adherence and emotions per trade.
  • Weekly reviews reveal your few repeated mistakes — fix those.
  • Simple and sustained beats fancy and abandoned.

Frequently asked questions

Paper or digital journal?

Whichever you will actually maintain. Spreadsheets make statistics easier; notebooks feel more reflective.

How long until journaling pays off?

Patterns emerge within a month; real behavioural change takes a few months of honest reviews.

Should I journal demo trades?

Absolutely — that is where the habit should be built, when nothing is at stake.
