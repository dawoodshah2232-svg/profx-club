Forex Pairs Explained: Majors, Minors, Exotics

September 29, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Currency pairs are quoted in a universal shorthand. Once you understand base and quote currencies, every pair on your screen starts to make sense.

Base and quote currency

In EUR/USD, the first currency (EUR) is the base and the second (USD) is the quote. The price tells you how much of the quote currency one unit of the base buys.

If EUR/USD is 1.0850, one euro buys 1.0850 US dollars. When the number rises, the base currency is strengthening.

Major pairs

Majors pair the US dollar with another heavily traded currency: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF and others.

They typically have the tightest spreads and deepest liquidity, which is why most beginners start here. Lower trading costs make learning cheaper.

Minor and exotic pairs

Minors are liquid pairs without the US dollar, like EUR/GBP or AUD/JPY.

Exotics pair a major currency with one from a smaller or emerging economy, such as USD/ZAR or EUR/TRY. Exotics usually have wider spreads and sharper moves, making them expensive and risky for beginners.

Which pairs should beginners watch?

Stick to one or two major pairs while learning.

Fewer pairs means fewer surprises and lower costs. Many beginners choose EUR/USD because it is liquid, well covered in educational material, and moves in relatively smooth trends.

Key takeaways

  • The price shows how much quote currency one unit of base buys.
  • Majors have the tightest spreads — best for beginners.
  • Exotics are costly and volatile; leave them until you are experienced.

Frequently asked questions

What does EUR/USD 1.0850 mean?

One euro buys 1.0850 US dollars. If the number rises to 1.0900, the euro strengthened.

Are exotic pairs bad?

Not bad, just expensive and volatile. Wider spreads and sudden moves punish small mistakes.

How many pairs should I follow?

One or two while learning. Depth beats breadth at the start.
