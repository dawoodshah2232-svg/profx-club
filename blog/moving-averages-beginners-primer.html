Moving Averages: A Beginner's Primer

August 26, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Moving averages smooth price into readable trend lines. They are the gentlest introduction to indicators — and genuinely useful.

What they do

A 50-period moving average shows the average price of the last 50 candles, updating each candle.

It filters noise: price above a rising MA suggests an uptrend; below a falling MA, a downtrend. Simple, visual, effective.

The popular ones

Beginners meet the 20/50/200-period MAs most.

Shorter MAs hug price closely (responsive, choppy); longer MAs are smooth (slow, reliable). The 200-period MA on daily charts is watched as long-term bull/bear territory by institutions.

Crossovers

When a fast MA crosses above a slow MA ('golden cross'), it signals strengthening momentum; crossing below ('death cross') signals weakening.

They lag by nature — treat them as trend confirmation, not precise entry triggers.

Honest limitations

MAs are useless in sideways markets, whipsawing with false signals.

They tell you what has happened, not what will. Use them to define trend direction and dynamic support/resistance — never as a standalone buy/sell oracle.

Key takeaways

  • MAs smooth noise and define trend direction visually.
  • Crossovers confirm momentum; they lag, so don't use them alone.
  • In sideways markets, MA signals whipsaw — stand aside.

Frequently asked questions

Simple or exponential MA?

EMA reacts faster to recent price; SMA is smoother. Try both on demo and see which suits your style.

Which periods should I use?

Common starter: 20 and 50 for trend, 200 for big-picture context. There is no magic setting.

Can MAs be support/resistance?

Price often reacts at major MAs — treat them as zones of interest, not exact lines.
