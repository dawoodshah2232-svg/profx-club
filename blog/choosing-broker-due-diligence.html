Choosing a Broker: A Due-Diligence Checklist

August 20, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

A good broker is invisible; a bad one is expensive. Run every candidate through this checklist before depositing.

Regulation first

Confirm the broker is licensed by a recognised regulator (FCA, ASIC, CySEC, DFSA, SCA, etc.) — on the regulator's own website, not the broker's.

Regulation means segregated funds, fair conduct rules, and somewhere to complain. Unregulated = your money is a gift.

Costs: spreads and commissions

Compare typical spreads on the pairs you will trade, plus any commission per lot.

Small per-trade differences compound into serious money over hundreds of trades. Watch for 'zero spread' marketing that hides markups elsewhere.

Withdrawals and support

Search for withdrawal complaints before depositing, test with small amounts first, and contact support with a question — slow or evasive support pre-deposit means worse post-deposit.

Also confirm negative balance protection.

Platform and extras

Ensure they offer the platform you want (MT4/MT5), stable mobile apps, and demo accounts.

Bonuses and 'account managers' pushing you to deposit more are red flags, not perks.

Key takeaways

  • Verify the licence on the regulator's register — never the broker's word.
  • Compare all-in costs and test withdrawals with small sums.
  • Bonuses and pushy account managers are red flags.

Frequently asked questions

Which regulator is best?

Tier-one regulators (FCA, ASIC) are strictest; several others are solid. What matters most is that the licence is real and verifiable.

Are offshore brokers always bad?

Not always, but risk is higher and recourse weaker. Beginners should prefer well-regulated options.

Can I switch brokers later?

Yes — withdrawing and moving is normal. Never feel locked in.
