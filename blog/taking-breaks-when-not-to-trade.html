Taking Breaks: When Not to Trade

September 07, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Professionals sit out more than they trade. Knowing when not to trade protects both capital and confidence.

Bad market conditions

Skip major news releases until you understand them, avoid dead holiday sessions with wide spreads, and stand aside when your pair is chopping sideways with no clear direction.

Forcing trades in bad conditions is paying to practise losing.

Bad personal conditions

Tired, ill, emotional, distracted, or trading to 'make back' something — all are stop signals.

Your edge assumes a clear head; without it, you are donating. Write personal stop conditions into your plan next to the market ones.

Scheduled breaks

Build rest into your routine: no trading on certain days, a week off each quarter, screen-free weekends.

Distance restores perspective — many traders find their best insights arrive away from charts. Burnout makes every decision worse.

What to do instead

Review your journal, study one concept deeply, backtest an idea, or simply rest.

Breaks are not lost opportunity — the market's infinite opportunity will still be there tomorrow, and you will meet it sharper.

Key takeaways

  • Skip news spikes, dead sessions and choppy markets.
  • Skip trading when tired, emotional or distracted.
  • Schedule regular breaks — rest is part of the strategy.

Frequently asked questions

Won't I miss opportunities by taking breaks?

You will miss some — and avoid far more losses. Net, breaks pay.

How long should a break be?

From an afternoon after hitting your loss limit, to a week each quarter. Match the break to the cause.

What if trading is my income?

Then breaks are even more critical — professionals in every field schedule recovery. Exhausted traders make expensive errors.
