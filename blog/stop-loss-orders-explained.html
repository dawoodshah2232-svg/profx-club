Stop-Loss Orders: Your Safety Net Explained

September 21, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

A stop-loss is a pre-set order that closes your trade if the price moves against you. It is the single most important order type for beginners.

How stop-losses work

You buy EUR/USD at 1.0850 and set a stop-loss at 1.0800.

If price falls to 1.0800, your position closes automatically for a 50-pip loss — no watching, no hoping. The stop turns an unlimited risk into a known, planned cost.

Where to place them

Place stops where your trade idea is proven wrong — beyond a support/resistance level or a logical technical point — not at a random pip count.

A stop inside normal market noise gets hit by meaningless wiggles; a stop with no logic is just a delayed loss.

Common stop-loss mistakes

Moving your stop further away when price approaches it ('it will come back') destroys the entire purpose.

Setting stops so tight that normal volatility hits them guarantees death by a thousand cuts. And trading without any stop is not brave — it is how accounts blow up.

Stops and position size together

Your stop distance and position size are one decision: together they define your risk in money.

A 100-pip stop with a tiny size can risk less than a 10-pip stop with a huge size. Always calculate both before entering.

Key takeaways

  • A stop-loss converts unlimited risk into a planned, known cost.
  • Place stops where your idea is proven wrong, beyond market noise.
  • Never move a stop further away to avoid a loss.

Frequently asked questions

Can my stop-loss be skipped in fast markets?

Yes — in gaps or extreme volatility, fills can slip past your stop price. Size positions so even slippage is survivable.

Should I use a mental stop instead?

No. Mental stops fail exactly when you need them — under emotional pressure. Always place the real order.

What about take-profit orders?

Use them too. Deciding your exit in advance for both wins and losses is what makes trading a plan instead of a gamble.
