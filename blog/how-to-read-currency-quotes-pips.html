Forex Basics

How to Read Currency Quotes and Pips

September 28, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Every forex quote shows two prices and a tiny unit called a pip. This guide decodes them so you always know what you are looking at.

Bid and ask

The bid is the price buyers will pay; the ask is the price sellers want. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid.

The difference between them is the spread — the broker's cut on every trade. Tighter spreads mean lower costs for you.

What is a pip?

A pip is the standard unit of price movement, usually the fourth decimal place (0.0001) for most pairs. If EUR/USD moves from 1.0850 to 1.0860, that is a 10-pip move.

For yen pairs, a pip is the second decimal place (0.01). Pips let traders talk about moves without quoting full prices.

What is a pip worth?

A pip's cash value depends on your position size.

On a standard lot (100,000 units), one pip on EUR/USD is roughly $10; on a mini lot (10,000) it is roughly $1; on a micro lot (1,000) roughly $0.10. This is why position sizing matters so much — the same 20-pip move can mean $2 or $200.

Reading a quote in practice

Take EUR/USD bid 1.0848 / ask 1.0850. The spread is 2 pips.

If you buy and the price rises 10 pips, you are roughly 8 pips in profit after the spread. Always factor the spread into your plan — it is a cost you pay on every single trade.

Key takeaways

  • Buy at the ask, sell at the bid; the gap is the spread.
  • A pip is the standard unit of movement — usually 0.0001.
  • Pip value depends on position size: control size, control risk.

Frequently asked questions

Why do yen pairs count pips differently?

Because USD/JPY is quoted to two decimals (e.g. 149.50), so one pip is 0.01 instead of 0.0001.

Is the spread the same all day?

No. Spreads widen during news events and quiet hours, and tighten during busy sessions.

Do pips matter for gold (XAUUSD)?

Gold is usually quoted to two decimals, so traders talk in points or cents rather than classic pips.
