Margin Calls: What They Are and How to Avoid Them

September 18, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Margin calls are the market's way of telling you a trade went badly wrong. Here is what they are and the simple habits that prevent them.

The mechanics

When you open a leveraged trade, the broker locks part of your balance as margin.

If losses eat into your remaining free margin past a threshold (often 100%), you get a margin call: deposit more or the broker starts closing your positions automatically (stop-out). It is not a negotiation.

What triggers them

Oversized positions, no stop-loss, adding to losers, and holding through violent news — usually in combination.

The common thread is risking too much on too little certainty. A margin call is never bad luck; it is the bill for earlier decisions.

How to avoid them forever

Risk 1% or less per trade, always use stop-losses, keep plenty of free margin (never max out your buying power), and avoid holding leveraged positions through major news.

If you follow a real trading plan, margin calls become a story other people tell.

If you ever get one

Close or reduce positions immediately to free margin — do not deposit more to 'save' a bad trade.

Then stop trading, review your journal, and fix the sizing error that caused it. A margin call is tuition; make sure you actually learn the lesson.

Key takeaways

  • A margin call = deposit more or the broker closes your trades.
  • Caused by oversized positions and missing stop-losses — never bad luck.
  • Risk 1%, use stops, keep free margin high: calls become impossible.

Frequently asked questions

Will the broker always warn me first?

Most issue a margin call warning before stop-out, but in fast markets positions can be closed with little notice.

Can I lose more than my deposit?

Reputable regulated brokers offer negative balance protection — verify yours does.

Is trading without leverage safer?

Yes, dramatically — but even unleveraged, never risk money you cannot afford to lose.
