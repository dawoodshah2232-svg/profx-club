Position Sizing 101: Risk Only What You Can Lose

September 22, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Most beginners obsess over entries and ignore position size. Yet size — not strategy — decides whether a normal losing streak ruins you.

The core formula

Decide your risk per trade first (many use 1% of the account), then size the position so your stop-loss equals that amount. Example: $1,000 account, 1% risk = $10.

With a 50-pip stop on EUR/USD, you need a size where 50 pips ≈ $10 — about a micro lot. The stop distance sets the size, never the other way round.

Why fixed sizes fail

Trading the same lot size on every trade ignores that stops differ.

A 20-pip stop and a 100-pip stop with the same lot size risk wildly different amounts. Always recalculate size from your stop distance — it takes ten seconds on a position-size calculator.

Risk per trade guidelines

Beginners commonly risk 0.5–1% per trade; experienced traders rarely exceed 2%.

This means even ten straight losses cost only 5–10% of the account — survivable. Risk 10% per trade and three losses wipe out over a quarter of your money.

Putting it into practice

Before every trade, write down: account size, risk %, stop distance in pips, and the resulting lot size.

If the math gives an awkward size, round down. Undersizing a winner costs a little profit; oversizing a loser can end your account.

Key takeaways

  • Risk % first, then derive size from your stop distance.
  • 0.5–1% per trade keeps losing streaks survivable.
  • Round position sizes down when in doubt.

Frequently asked questions

What is a position-size calculator?

A free tool where you enter account size, risk % and stop distance, and it outputs the correct lot size.

Should I increase size after wins?

Not automatically. Increase only when your account grows and your plan allows — never on emotion.

What if my broker's minimum size is too big?

Then your account is too small for that broker or pair — use a smaller account type, a different pair, or keep practising on demo.
