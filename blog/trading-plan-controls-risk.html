Risk Management

Building a Trading Plan That Controls Risk

September 15, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

A trading plan is a written set of rules for what you trade, when, and how much you risk. Traders with plans outperform traders with impulses.

What goes in the plan

Markets and sessions you trade, your exact entry setup, stop-loss and take-profit rules, risk per trade (e.g. 1%), maximum daily loss, and when you stop for the day.

If a situation is not in the plan, you do not trade it. Vagueness is where discipline dies.

The daily loss limit

This is your emergency brake: e.g. stop trading after losing 3% in a day.

It prevents one bad morning from becoming a catastrophic day. Professionals treat this rule as sacred — it has saved more accounts than any strategy.

Keep it to one page

A plan you will not read is decoration.

One page of clear rules beats a twenty-page manual you ignore. Review it before each session; tick off that you read it in your journal.

Review monthly

Each month, compare your trades against the plan.

Score yourself on process (did I follow rules?) not just profit. Update the plan only from journal evidence, never from a single emotional trade.

Key takeaways

  • Write down markets, setups, risk %, and daily loss limits.
  • One clear page you actually read beats a long ignored manual.
  • Review monthly on process adherence, not just profit.

Frequently asked questions

Can I copy someone else's trading plan?

You can borrow structure, but risk rules must fit your account size and temperament.

How strict should the plan be?

Strict on risk, flexible enough to skip unclear setups. Discretion within rules is fine; breaking rules is not.

Where do I start writing one?

List your risk rules first — they matter more than entry tactics. ProFX Club's week-three class covers this.
