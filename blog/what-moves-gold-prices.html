Gold Trading

What Moves Gold Prices?

September 04, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Gold does not move randomly. A handful of macro forces explain most of its major trends.

Real interest rates

Gold pays no interest, so when real rates (rates minus inflation) rise, holding gold has a higher opportunity cost and prices often fall.

When real rates fall, gold shines. This is the single most important gold driver to understand.

The US dollar

Gold is priced in dollars, so a weaker dollar mechanically lifts gold and a stronger dollar presses it.

Watch the dollar index (DXY) alongside XAUUSD — they usually mirror each other.

Inflation and uncertainty

Gold is the classic inflation hedge and crisis asset.

When inflation fears or geopolitical shocks rise, money flows into gold. These flows can overpower everything else for weeks — respect the trend they create.

Central bank buying

Central banks buy hundreds of tonnes of gold yearly, creating steady underlying demand.

You do not need to track every purchase — just know there is a persistent bid beneath the market that supports long-term uptrends.

Key takeaways

  • Falling real rates and a weak dollar lift gold; the reverse presses it.
  • Crises and inflation fears trigger strong safe-haven flows.
  • Central-bank demand provides long-term underlying support.

Frequently asked questions

Why did gold spike on that news event?

Likely a rush to safety or a shift in rate expectations — check which driver the headlines cite.

Does gold always rise in crises?

Usually initially, but nothing is guaranteed — sharp reversals happen when panic fades.

Should beginners trade gold news?

No. Watch a few releases on demo first; gold's news spikes are violent.
