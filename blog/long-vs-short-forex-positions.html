Long vs Short: How Forex Positions Work

September 25, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

In forex you can profit from prices rising or falling. Understanding long and short positions is the foundation of every trade you will ever place.

Going long

Going long means buying the pair, expecting the base currency to strengthen.

Buy EUR/USD at 1.0850 and sell later at 1.0950, and you profit from the 100-pip rise. Your risk is the price falling instead — which is why every long needs a planned exit if you are wrong.

Going short

Going short means selling the pair first, expecting the base currency to weaken.

Short EUR/USD at 1.0850 and buy back at 1.0750 to profit from the 100-pip fall. Unlike stock markets, shorting in forex is as easy as buying — no borrowing needed.

How profit is calculated

Profit or loss = (exit price − entry price) × position size, minus the spread and any fees.

A 50-pip win on a micro lot might be $5; on a standard lot, $500. Same market move, wildly different outcomes — position size is the lever that sets your risk.

The beginner trap

New traders often go long simply because a price 'looks cheap' or short because it 'looks expensive', with no plan.

Every position needs three things decided in advance: where you are wrong (stop-loss), where you take profit, and how much you risk. No plan, no trade.

Key takeaways

  • Long profits from rising prices; short profits from falling prices.
  • Shorting forex is as simple as buying — no borrowing involved.
  • Decide your stop, target and risk before entering any position.

Frequently asked questions

Can I lose more than I deposit?

With most regulated retail brokers offering negative balance protection, no — but always confirm this with your broker.

Is shorting riskier than going long?

In forex, no — both directions carry the same mechanics. Risk comes from size and leverage, not direction.

How do I close a position?

You close by taking the opposite action: sell what you bought, or buy back what you sold.
