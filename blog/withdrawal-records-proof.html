Withdrawal Records: Keep Proof of Everything

August 18, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Keeping transaction records is unglamorous and invaluable — the one habit that protects you if anything goes wrong.

What to keep

Every deposit and withdrawal: date, amount, method, reference/transaction ID, and screenshots of confirmations.

Also save your broker's terms, fee schedules, and any support chats about money. Store copies outside the broker's portal — email them to yourself.

Why it matters

Records resolve disputes: 'here is my withdrawal request #12345 from March 3rd, still pending' beats 'you owe me money'.

They also simplify your own accounting and any tax questions. Professionals document everything; amateurs rely on memory.

Test withdrawals early

Withdraw a small amount soon after your first deposit to verify the process works and learn the timeline.

A broker that processes a $50 withdrawal smoothly earns trust; one that stalls on $50 will stall on $5,000.

If a withdrawal stalls

Contact support in writing, escalate with your reference numbers, and if unresolved, complain to the regulator with your full paper trail.

Document every step. This process only works if you kept records from day one.

Key takeaways

  • Save every transaction's date, amount, method and reference ID.
  • Test with a small withdrawal before trusting larger sums.
  • Written records + reference numbers win disputes.

Frequently asked questions

How long should withdrawals take?

Reputable brokers typically process within 1–5 business days depending on method.

What is a withdrawal red flag?

Repeated 'verification' delays, new fees appearing, or support going silent.

Should I keep records of demo trading?

Journal demo trades for learning; keep financial records for all real-money movements.
