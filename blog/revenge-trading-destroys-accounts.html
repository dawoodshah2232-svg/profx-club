Revenge Trading: Why It Destroys Accounts

September 13, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Revenge trading is entering impulsive trades to recover a loss quickly. It turns a normal losing trade into a disaster.

The revenge spiral

You lose a trade. Anger says the market 'owes' you. You double your size on the next trade — no setup, no plan.

It loses too. Now you are down twice as much and twice as emotional. This spiral, not any single loss, is how most accounts die.

Why it feels so compelling

Losses trigger the same brain circuits as physical threats; fighting back feels like survival.

But the market is not an opponent — it does not know you exist. 'Getting even' with a price chart is like arguing with the weather.

Breaking the cycle

Your trading plan needs a circuit breaker: after two consecutive losses or a set daily loss, you stop for the day — no exceptions.

Physically leave the screen. The market will be there tomorrow; your account might not be if you continue today.

Reframe the loss

A planned 1% loss is a business expense, like rent for a shop. You would not burn down the shop because of one month's rent.

Journal the loss, note what you will review, and move on. Professionals lose constantly — they just lose small and on-plan.

Key takeaways

  • Revenge trading turns one planned loss into an account-ending spiral.
  • The market is not your opponent — there is nothing to 'win back'.
  • Use a circuit breaker: stop trading after set consecutive/daily losses.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know I'm revenge trading?

Signs: bigger size than planned, no clear setup, urgency, anger. If in doubt, you are. Stop.

Is it ever okay to trade right after a loss?

Only if the next trade was already planned with full setup and normal size — never as a reaction.

What should I do instead?

Close the platform, journal the loss, exercise or rest. Review tomorrow with a clear head.
