Risk Management

Drawdowns: Surviving Losing Streaks

September 16, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Drawdown is the dip from your account's peak to its lowest point. Understanding it is the difference between a bad month and a blown account.

Drawdown is normal

Even excellent strategies lose 5–10 trades in a row. With 1% risk per trade, that is a 5–10% drawdown — uncomfortable but recoverable.

Expecting to avoid drawdowns is expecting to avoid reality. Plan for them instead.

The math gets brutal

Lose 50% and you need 100% just to break even. Lose 20% and you need 25%.

This asymmetry is why professionals guard capital so fiercely: deep holes are exponentially harder to climb out of. Small risks keep drawdowns shallow.

Rules for drawdown periods

Set a personal circuit breaker: at a 10% drawdown, halve your risk; at 15%, stop trading for a week and review.

Never increase size to 'win it back' — that is how 10% becomes 50%. Write these rules down before you need them.

Review, don't despair

During drawdowns, review your journal for process errors versus normal variance. If you followed your plan and still lost, that is the cost of doing business.

If you broke rules, fix the behaviour. Either way, the journal — not your emotions — gives the verdict.

Key takeaways

  • Losing streaks are normal; plan for them in advance.
  • Deep drawdowns need exponential gains to recover — stay shallow.
  • Use circuit breakers: reduce size or stop at set drawdown levels.

Frequently asked questions

What is an acceptable drawdown?

There is no universal number, but consistently staying under 10–15% suggests sane risk management.

Should I stop trading during a drawdown?

Pausing to review is wise. Quitting forever after normal variance is not — let the journal decide.

How do I recover from a big drawdown?

Slowly, with reduced size and strict process. Aggressive recovery attempts usually deepen the hole.
