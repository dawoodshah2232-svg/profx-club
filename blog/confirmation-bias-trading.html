Confirmation Bias: Seeing What You Want

September 09, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Confirmation bias makes you notice bullish signs when you are long and ignore bearish ones. It is silent, universal, and expensive.

How it sabotages trades

You buy EUR/USD, then every small bounce 'confirms' your genius while you dismiss the clear resistance above.

You hold too long, skip your stop, and turn a planned small loss into a big one. The bias does not change the market — it changes what you are willing to see.

Argue against yourself

Before entering, write one paragraph on why the trade could fail.

If you cannot find three bearish points for your long, you are not analysing — you are hoping. Professionals actively try to destroy their own trade ideas; survivors get traded.

Use checklists, not feelings

A written entry checklist (trend? level? confirmation? risk-reward?) forces you to verify each condition objectively.

Feelings say 'this looks good'; checklists ask 'which rule, exactly?' Rules are bias-resistant.

Seek disconfirming opinions

Discuss setups with other traders and invite criticism — this is one reason trading communities help.

A trading buddy who asks 'but what about resistance at X?' is worth more than ten cheerleaders.

Key takeaways

  • You naturally overweight evidence supporting your position.
  • Write the bear case for every bull trade (and vice versa).
  • Checklists and critical peers counteract the bias.

Frequently asked questions

Is confirmation bias the same as being optimistic?

Related but different: bias filters information, optimism colours mood. Both hurt, but bias specifically corrupts analysis.

Can indicators remove bias?

They help by adding objective criteria, but you can still cherry-pick indicators — hence checklists.

Does experience eliminate it?

No, but experienced traders build systems that contain it. The bias stays; the defences improve.
