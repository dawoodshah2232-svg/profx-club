Risk Management

The 1% Risk Rule Every Beginner Should Know

September 20, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

The 1% rule is the most repeated advice in trading education — because it works. Here is the math that makes it powerful.

What the rule says

Never risk more than 1% of your account balance on a single trade.

On a $1,000 account, that is $10 per trade. It does not limit your profit — it limits how much damage any one idea can do.

The math of survival

Lose 10 trades in a row at 1% risk and you are down about 9.5% — annoying but recoverable.

Lose 10 in a row risking 10% each and you are down 65% — nearly finished. Losing streaks happen to everyone; the 1% rule makes them survivable.

It buys you time to learn

Beginners pay 'tuition' to the market through mistakes.

The 1% rule stretches that tuition over hundreds of trades instead of a handful, giving you time to actually improve. Blowing up in week two teaches nothing except regret.

When to adjust

Some experienced traders use 2%; almost none go above it.

As a beginner, 1% (or even 0.5%) is the sweet spot. Only consider changing it after a year of journaled, disciplined results — not after a lucky week.

Key takeaways

  • 1% risk per trade makes losing streaks survivable.
  • It stretches your learning budget over hundreds of trades.
  • Stay at 1% or less until you have a year of disciplined results.

Frequently asked questions

Does 1% mean I can only make tiny profits?

Profits scale with account growth over time. Small, consistent gains compound; big bets blow up.

What if 1% is less than the minimum position?

Then the account or broker is wrong for you — practise on demo or choose a smaller account type.

Do professionals really risk so little?

Yes. Professional risk managers think in terms of survival first; returns follow.
