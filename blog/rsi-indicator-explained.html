RSI Indicator: What It Tells You

August 25, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

RSI measures the speed of price moves on a 0–100 scale. It is popular, useful, and widely misused — here is the balanced view.

How RSI works

RSI compares recent gains to recent losses.

Readings above 70 suggest overbought (rising fast, maybe exhausted); below 30 oversold (falling fast, maybe due a bounce). In strong trends, RSI can stay extreme for a long time — 'overbought' is not a sell signal by itself.

The best beginner use: divergence

When price makes a new high but RSI makes a lower high, momentum is fading — a divergence that often precedes reversals.

Divergence at support/resistance zones is one of the more reliable signals RSI offers.

Common misuses

Selling just because RSI hit 70 in a roaring uptrend is a classic beginner donation.

RSI describes momentum; it does not predict tops. Always combine it with price structure — indicator plus context, never indicator alone.

Settings and practice

The default 14-period setting suits most beginners.

Practise spotting divergences on historical charts before trading them live — your eyes need training to see them in real time.

Key takeaways

  • RSI gauges momentum speed; extremes can persist in strong trends.
  • Divergence (price vs RSI) is its most useful signal.
  • Combine with price structure — never trade RSI alone.

Frequently asked questions

What RSI setting is best?

14 is the standard starting point. Shorter is twitchier, longer is smoother.

Does RSI work on all timeframes?

Yes, but signals on higher timeframes carry more weight.

Overbought means sell, right?

No — in strong uptrends RSI stays overbought for ages. Wait for price confirmation.
