Spreads and Commissions Explained

August 07, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Trading costs are small per trade and enormous over time. Understanding them is pure profit protection.

Spread: the hidden cost

The spread (ask minus bid) is paid on every trade — you start each position slightly underwater.

On EUR/USD a 1-pip spread on a mini lot costs about $1; trade 500 times a year and that is $500 regardless of results.

Commission models

Some brokers charge 'zero spread' plus a commission per lot (e.g. $3–$7 per lot round-turn).

Compare all-in costs: spread-only vs spread-plus-commission for your typical size. Marketing highlights one number; your maths should include both.

Why costs shape strategy

High costs punish frequent trading: scalping tiny moves with wide spreads is nearly impossible profitably.

This is one reason beginners do better on higher timeframes — fewer trades, lower cost drag, more room for each idea.

Minimising costs

Trade liquid majors in liquid sessions (tightest spreads), avoid news spikes (spreads balloon), compare brokers' all-in costs annually, and reduce trade frequency to high-quality setups.

Every pip saved is a pip earned.

Key takeaways

  • Spreads + commissions compound into serious yearly costs.
  • High costs punish overtrading — another reason for selectivity.
  • Trade liquid pairs in liquid sessions; avoid news-time spreads.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good EUR/USD spread?

Often under 1 pip at liquid times with decent brokers; beware much wider ones.

Zero-spread accounts — catch?

They charge commission instead. Compare total cost per lot, not headlines.

Do costs apply on demo?

Demo usually mirrors spreads, so your cost-awareness transfers to live.
