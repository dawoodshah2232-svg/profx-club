Forex Basics

Forex Jargon Buster: 25 Terms to Know

September 24, 2026 · 2 min read · By ProFX Club

Forex has its own language. Here are the terms you will see daily, translated into plain English.

Prices and costs

Pip: the standard unit of price movement. Spread: the gap between bid and ask — your per-trade cost.

Slippage: when your order fills at a worse price than expected, common in fast markets. Commission: a separate fee some brokers charge per trade. Swap: overnight interest credited or charged for holding past 22:00 UTC.

Sizes and leverage

Lot: the standardised trade size (standard 100,000 units, mini 10,000, micro 1,000). Leverage: borrowed buying power, e.g. 1:100.

Margin: the deposit your broker locks up to support a leveraged position. Margin call: a warning that your funds are running low. Stop-out: the broker forcibly closing positions to prevent further loss.

Orders and risk

Market order: buy/sell instantly at the current price. Limit order: buy/sell only at your chosen price or better. Stop-loss: an order that exits a losing trade automatically.

Take-profit: an order that locks in gains at your target. Drawdown: the peak-to-trough fall of your account. Equity: your balance plus open profit/loss.

How to actually learn them

Do not memorise — use them.

Open a demo account and place trades using each order type; the terms stick when tied to actions. Keep this page bookmarked and revisit it whenever a new word appears in your classes.

Key takeaways

  • Learn prices/costs, sizes/leverage, and orders/risk as three groups.
  • Use terms on a demo account — context beats memorisation.
  • Bookmark a glossary and revisit it as you learn.

Frequently asked questions

What is the single most important term?

Risk-related ones: stop-loss, position size and margin. They protect your account.

Do I need to know all 25 before starting?

No — learn the basics, then pick up the rest as you practise.
